For mod authors
API version 1. This part of the readme is the whole API, nothing else of the mod belongs to it.

In short
  Open someone's inventory the game's way and the panel shows them instead of the player:
    akActor.OpenInventory(true)
  That's the container menu in its NPC mode, the one of a follower's "Let's trade some things". A follower
  framework that opens a follower's inventory this way gets the panel with no code at all: their doll, what they
  wear in the slots, and what the player drags into a slot goes on them. The calls below are for a mod that wants
  to know what the player changed, or to keep the panel's hands off an actor for a while.

Who gets the panel
  A living actor whose race has the ActorTypeNPC keyword, enabled, loaded in the world, with their AI on, a
  teammate or not. Everyone else gets the plain container menu: creatures, the dead, the
  disabled, someone in an unloaded cell (a "remote" inventory), mannequins and posed statues (AI off), a chest
  standing in for someone, the gift menu (ShowGiftMenu) and shops. ShowsPanelFor tells it before
  you open the trade. The player can switch it off ("Panel in a follower's trade", [Panel] bFollowers).

What the panel does to the actor
  Pieces of their default outfit stand in the slots too, though the game keeps them out of the trade's rows. A
  piece of it they carry and don't wear loses the outfit mark (ExtraOutfitItem) as their trade opens, or as the
  panel takes it off or puts another piece in its place: from then on it's an ordinary item of theirs with its own
  row, and the game adds no second set of that outfit when it re-dresses them. Pieces of a sleep outfit keep it.
  A row of the player's side dropped into a slot is given first through the menu's own move, so the game
  checks it as any gift, then it goes on. A trade closed in that moment keeps the gift and puts nothing on.
  The panel equips with the flags of EquipItem(akItem, abPreventRemoval = false) and unequips with those of
  UnequipItem(akItem, abPreventEquip = true). What stood in the place of a new piece comes off first.
  While "Followers keep what you give" ([Panel] bKeepChoices) is on, the player's choice holds: the actor's combat
  AI doesn't pick a rival of a chosen piece, the game's own re-dress when their inventory changes, on a cell
  change or a load puts nothing over it, and a change by anyone else goes back after a second. A script that
  changes the same piece back three times in ten minutes keeps that piece, the rest of the choice holds. Off, the
  game dresses them its own way. SetReadOnly keeps all of it off an actor.

Papyrus: script InventoryReforged, all global
  The source is Source/Scripts/InventoryReforged.psc. Compile against it, never pack InventoryReforged.pex in
  your mod: an older copy would win over the mod's newer one. The functions at its top are the mod's own menu.

  Int GetApiVersion()                       1 now
  Actor GetPanelActor()                     who the panel shows now: the player in the inventory, the actor in
                                            their trade. None with no panel up, and already in OnMenuClose
  Bool ShowsPanelFor(Actor akActor)         whether OpenInventory on this actor would show the panel now
  SetReadOnly(Actor akActor, Bool abReadOnly)
                                            True: the panel shows the actor in their trade but puts nothing on
                                            and takes nothing off; giving and taking items stays the game's. For a
                                            quest's disguise or while your script switches their gear. At once,
                                            also in a trade already open. Kept in the save until set back to False.
                                            The player is never read only. Up to 256 actors. The mod's menu has a
                                            button that clears all of them, for a mod that's gone
  RegisterForWear(Form akReceiver)
  UnregisterForWear(Form akReceiver)        the script on akReceiver gets the two events below for every actor.
                                            Twice is once. Kept in the save. Register one quest or a few forms,
                                            never every actor: 16 per plugin file, and the forms made while the
                                            game runs share one place of 16 among all mods

  Events, written as Functions (Caprica refuses Events of names the game doesn't know, the game calls these
  the same way):
    Function OnInventoryReforgedWear(Actor akWearer, Form akItem, Bool abOn)
      The player put a piece on someone through the panel (abOn true) or took one off (false), after the game
      did it. A piece pushed off for a new one comes as taken off, before the new one. A piece the game refused
      never comes, nor does what the AI or a script equips. The player's own inventory sends nothing.
    Function OnInventoryReforgedDone(Actor akWearer, Bool abChanged)
      The trade with the panel closed, after its last OnInventoryReforgedWear. abChanged when any came. The
      place to save a set of gear once.
  The actor's own OnObjectEquipped comes before the panel's event of the same piece. Each event runs as its own
  Papyrus call: two may interleave when a handler calls something that waits.

  A whole listener, on a quest of your mod:
    Scriptname MyFollowerGear extends Quest
    Event OnInit()
      If SKSE.GetPluginVersion("InventoryReforged") > 0
        InventoryReforged.RegisterForWear(self)
      EndIf
    EndEvent
    Function OnInventoryReforgedWear(Actor akWearer, Form akItem, Bool abOn)
      ; a ReferenceAlias isn't a Form, find the alias that holds akWearer here and hand the event on
    EndFunction
    Function OnInventoryReforgedDone(Actor akWearer, Bool abChanged)
      If abChanged
        ; save akWearer's gear set
      EndIf
    EndFunction
  A quest of a mod already running when Inventory Reforged is installed has had its OnInit: register again from
  OnPlayerLoadGame of a ReferenceAlias on the player, that's harmless.

What stays the same
  The functions and events above with their names, arguments and meaning, and the behaviour described here.
  A later version adds and raises the version number, it takes nothing away. Anything else of the mod is not
  part of the API.

Good to know
  Numbers in the list, the item window and the arrows of rows are the game's numbers for the player, on the
  actor's rows too: a rough guide for them.
  A script's equip of the actor during their trade shows at once on the doll and in the slots, except on one
  riding, sitting, knocked down or bleeding out: their doll waits for the game's own update.
